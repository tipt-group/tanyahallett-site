Proprietary Model

The Grounded Trust Framework

A model for building psychologically safe teams from the nervous system up: four pillars, in sequence, that move a group from surviving pressure to actually trusting one another under it.

Bring This to Your Organisation

Most frameworks treat psychological safety as a single dial to turn up. The Grounded Trust Framework treats it as four separable, sequential conditions, each with its own diagnostic questions, common failure points, and practical exercises. Teams don't move to Voice before they've built Trust, and they don't sustain Trust without first being Grounded.

The Four Pillars

Ground. Trust. Voice. Repair.

01

Ground

Establish physiological and relational safety before anything else.

  • Recognise stress-response states in yourself and others before responding to them
  • Build predictable rhythms and rituals into team life
  • Separate genuine threat from manufactured urgency
In practice: teams learn to name activation in the moment ("I'm noticing we've gone into fight mode") before a decision gets made from that state.
02

Trust

Build relational credibility through consistency, not charisma.

  • Match words to actions, visibly and often
  • Make power and decision-making processes transparent
  • Treat small commitments with the same seriousness as big ones
In practice: leaders run a simple behavioural audit (where do my stated values and my actual decisions diverge?) and share the results with their team.
03

Voice

Create real structures for participation, dissent, and disclosure.

  • Separate the act of speaking up from the risk of being punished for it
  • Build more than one channel for feedback to travel through
  • Normalise disagreement as a sign of a healthy, not broken, team
In practice: teams pilot a standing agenda item ("what are we not saying?") as a structured, low-stakes way to surface dissent early.
04

Repair

Normalise rupture and treat repair as a core leadership skill, not a crisis response.

  • Name harm directly and without defensiveness
  • Separate accountability from punishment
  • Build repair rituals in advance, rather than improvising an apology after the fact
In practice: leaders rehearse a structured repair conversation before conflict happens, so the model is already familiar when it's actually needed.
Foundations

Where this framework comes from

+61 424 988 052 The Grounded Trust Framework is grounded in established trauma-informed care principles (safety, trustworthiness, choice, collaboration, and empowerment) adapted from clinical and community settings for organisational and leadership contexts. It draws on interpersonal neurobiology and polyvagal-informed practice to explain why these conditions matter physiologically, not just culturally, and on Tanya's own facilitation experience to specify how they show up and break down in real teams.

The framework is taught as a standalone keynote, as a half-day workshop, and as the backbone of TIPT's longer embedded training programmes.
