Philosophy

How Tanya works

Three commitments shape every keynote, workshop, and framework Tanya brings into a room.

Safety is built, not declared

A values poster does not change what happens in a performance review, a restructure conversation, or a Monday stand-up. Tanya works from observable conditions and behaviours — the things a team can actually change — rather than sentiment.

Nervous systems before mission statements

People in a threat state cannot access the collaborative, creative thinking most workplaces need from them. Before culture change is possible, teams need to understand — and work with — what's happening physiologically under pressure.

Repair is a competency, not a crisis

Ruptures — a sharp email, a missed commitment, an exclusion — are inevitable in any team. What separates safe workplaces from unsafe ones is not the absence of rupture, but a practiced, non-punitive way of repairing it.

Why This Work

Why trauma-informed practice, why now

[Placeholder] Workplace psychological safety research and trauma-informed care have developed largely in parallel — one in organisational psychology, the other in clinical and community practice. Tanya's work sits deliberately at that intersection.

Organisations are increasingly aware that people bring their whole nervous systems to work, not just their job descriptions — and that leadership behaviour can either compound harm or actively support recovery. Trauma-informed practice offers a rigorous, well-evidenced language for that reality; psychological safety research offers the workplace outcomes leaders are already accountable for. Tanya's contribution is translation: taking care-sector-grade thinking about safety and trust and making it usable by people who lead teams, not clinics.
